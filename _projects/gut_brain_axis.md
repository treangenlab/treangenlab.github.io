## About the project
The gut and the brain are in constant communication, and an injury to one leaves a mark on the other. In a long-running collaboration with Dr. Sonia Villapol's lab at Houston Methodist Research Institute, the Treangen lab uses metagenomics to follow how the gut microbiome changes after brain injury and whether it can be used to diagnose injury or to improve recovery.

The Treangen lab provides the microbiome profiling and computational analysis for these studies, using full-length 16S rRNA and long-read sequencing.

### Brain injury and Alzheimer's disease
*Microbiota-targeted approaches to resolve dysbiosis-induced AD neuropathology following brain injury* is led by Dr. Villapol. People living with Alzheimer's disease are at higher risk of falls and traumatic brain injury, and their recovery is often poor. The project studies the brain-gut-microbiota axis in mouse models, following how the gut community shifts after injury and in response to probiotic and antibiotic treatment, and works toward precision microbiota therapies that protect against neurodegeneration after brain injury {% cite soriano2022fecal %}.

### Concussion
Concussion is hard to diagnose objectively: it often leaves no trace on standard brain imaging. In a pilot study, 33 collegiate football players gave blood, stool and saliva samples over the course of a season {% cite soriano2022alterations %}. Nanopore 16S rRNA sequencing of the gut microbiome showed a drop in two bacterial species, *Eubacterium rectale* and *Anaerostipes hadrus*, in the players who had a diagnosed concussion. In 2024, Houston Methodist announced a larger two-year follow-up study sponsored by the NFL Players Association.
