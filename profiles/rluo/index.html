Rossie Luo

2nd Year Ph.D. student, SSPB, multiple genome alignment and genotype-to-phenotype analysis

strain-level analysis genome alignment metagenomics

Rossie (2nd year PhD student, SSPB) worked in the synthetic biology industry for two years before starting her PhD program in Systems, Synthetic and Physical Biology (SSPB) at Rice. Her current research focuses on microbial metagenomics with strain-level comparative analysis to identify potential virulence factors. Rossie is also working on expanding the features and applications of Parsnp, a fast and robust whole genome alignment tool. Her main interests are exploring the genotype-phenotype relationships while developing tools to enable and accelerate biological discoveries. In particular, Rossie wants to learn more about the impact of genetic differences on protein structure/function and ultimately the fitness of an organism in a specific environment.

Rossie is supported by the NIH NIAID Genomic Center for Infectious Diseases (U19AI144297).

She is the lead developer of Strainify, a strain-level abundance analysis tool for short-read metagenomics (Luo et al., 2025).

Outside of research, she enjoys nature, running and spending time with her dog.
